Gothic Body Paragraph Builder

Purpose

This page helps you draft one body paragraph for the Gothic Literary Criticism essay. Work through six moves, one at a time, then copy or download a draft to keep revising.

Use this page to

  • Start with a claim topic sentence tied to your thesis
  • Build the paragraph in six numbered moves, with optional sentence starters
  • Bring in one peer-reviewed critic, then copy or download a draft
Project 3 rule

Both critics write on the same story. Most students use one criticism type for both critics. If you want, you can use two different types instead.

Source rule

Each body section starts with your claim, then story evidence, then where a peer-reviewed critic supports, extends, or pushes back, then a tie to the thesis.

The six moves

  1. Claim (topic sentence tied to the thesis)
  2. Story evidence with a quote and MLA in-text citation
  3. Analysis
  4. Critic (peer-reviewed, with a signal phrase and citation)
  5. Critic connection (agree, extend, or push back, linked to the story evidence)
  6. Thesis tie

Optional: a public-domain practice example (not a student paper)

Public domain story

This practice uses Edgar Allan Poe's "The Raven," which is in the public domain. In the opening, the speaker sits "weak and weary" over "forgotten lore" when a tapping comes at the chamber door (Poe).

Example critic (not a real source)

Example critic (practice only, not a real published source) argues that the speaker is already listening for a voice that will confirm his grief, so the bird's "Nevermore" feels like an answer he expected. Do not copy this invented critic into a real essay. Use your own story evidence and a real peer-reviewed critic on the same story.
