Gothic Literary Criticism Studio

A practice room for Essay 3. Choose a primary Gothic text, locate two critics of different types, outline the interpretive thesis, then draft the literary criticism essay.

Practice path Practice tools Resources All essays

Purpose

Write a 1,500 to 2,000 word MLA literary criticism essay on one Gothic primary text. State a contestable interpretive thesis, support it with close reading, and engage at least two peer-reviewed critics from two different criticism types.

Assignment overview
  • 1,500–2,000 word MLA literary criticism essay on one Gothic primary text.
  • Contestable interpretive thesis; close reading; at least two peer-reviewed critics from two different criticism types; resistant evidence or alternative reading.
Required reading (primary — choose one)
  • Edgar Allan Poe, “The Raven”
  • Shirley Jackson, “The Lottery”
  • William Faulkner, “A Rose for Emily”
  • Or an approved focused comparison.
Starter criticisms (optional starters; verify full text)
  • Poe: Cook “The Raven and the Antebellum Culture of Bereavement”; Miller “Prosody, Media, and the Poetry of Edgar Allan Poe”; Teitelroit “Poe’s Raven Says More Than ‘Nevermore’”; Hasan et al. on gloominess and despair.
  • Jackson: Robinson on “The Lottery” and Holocaust literature; Bowers on Jim Crow America; Hakaraia with Empson; Hosseini-Maasoum et al. feminist analysis.
  • Faulkner: Fetterley (The Resisting Reader chapter); Nebeker “Emily’s Rose of Love…”; Blythe “The Chivalric Narrator…”; Burduck “Another View of Faulkner’s Narrator…”
