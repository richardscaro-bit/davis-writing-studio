### spring-monster-comparison-studio.html

LEAD: A practice room for the Monster Comparison essay. Choose two iconic monsters that share meaningful ground, apply the same criteria to both, and draft a short MLA comparison that ends in one clear insight. This room is essay-first; no character cards required here.

SOFT PURPOSE: Compare two iconic monsters from the same meaningful category using consistent criteria and one insight beyond a list. Aim for a 500 to 750 word MLA comparison (AI Level 2: support, not composition).

Sample essays intro: Read for structure and insight, not for copying. Notice shared ground, parallel criteria, and how the thesis goes past a simple list.

Comparison moves:
- Shared ground: name the category both monsters belong to (not “both are monsters” alone).
- Consistent criteria: apply the same 3 to 4 lenses to each figure.
- Insight: say what the split reveals about culture, fear, power, or audience.
- Audience: shape examples and tone for classmates or another named reader group.

How to use this studio · Practice path steps:
1 Pick two icons: Two monsters with enough depth to compare and a real shared category.
2 Map criteria: List similarities and differences, then keep only criteria that work for both.
3 Draft a thesis: One sentence that states the insight, not a catalog of traits.
4 Outline: Introduction, parallel body points, conclusion, toward 500 to 750 words MLA.
5 Peer rehearsal: Check insight, parallel structure, and evidence.
6 MLA polish: Practice in-text citations and Works Cited; verify every entry.

AI Level 2 note: AI may help brainstorm criteria or give reader-style feedback after you have your own notes or draft. AI may not write or rewrite your thesis, analysis, or finished essay.

### facing-the-unknown-studio.html

LEAD: A practice room for the Facing the Unknown research exposition. Pick a topic people find unknown, strange, or frightening (a real animal, problem, natural force, or idea), then explain it clearly and objectively. You inform; you do not argue. Use Tools for keywords, sources, outline, MLA, and peer rehearsal.

SOFT PURPOSE: Explain one focused how or why question about something unknown, strange, or frightening. Stay objective. Aim for a researched MLA exposition with credible sources, including SAC Library databases when your prompt asks for them (AI Level 2: support, not composition).

Exposition moves:
- Ask how, why, or what, not which side is right.
- Narrow until the question fits one essay (not “the ocean”; a focused survival, legend, or system question).
- Define terms, describe how something works, and explain causes or effects.
- Use signal phrases and explain each source; avoid quote stacking.
- Keep tone neutral; cut sentences that take a side.

Research help intro: Build keywords, log sources, and practice MLA here. Pair Scholar discovery with SAC Library access when your assignment requires database sources.

How to use · Practice path steps:
1 Choose and narrow: One Facing the Unknown option or focused question that informs, not persuades.
2 Search keywords: Turn the question into database and Scholar queries.
3 Log sources: Track what each source explains and how it helps your controlling idea.
4 Two-part thesis: Draft a controlling idea that previews what the essay will explain.
5 Outline by subtopics: Order definitions, process, causes, or effects for a college reader.
6 Peer and polish: Check objectivity, source jobs, transitions, and MLA.

AI Level 2 note: AI may support topic narrowing, keywords, or reader-style feedback after you begin with your own work. Source evaluation, integration, citations, and final prose stay yours.

### field-guide-studio.html

LEAD: A practice room for the face-to-face Field Guide project. Explain a place, practice, or local system for a named audience. Build one plate and a how/why essay that match each other. You explain; you do not take a side.

SOFT PURPOSE: Explain one focused how or why question about a place, practice, or local system for a named audience. Build one Field Guide plate plus a 750 to 1,000 word MLA essay with 1 to 3 credible sources (AI Level 2: support, not composition).

Plate labels intro: Fill every label before you draft the essay. The plate and the essay should describe the same place and audience.

Essay that matches the plate (cue lines):
- One how or why question (if it has should, shrink it; that is argument).
- Named audience.
- 1 to 3 sources a reader can open.
- 2 to 3 subtopics that match the plate.
- 750 to 1,000 words MLA plus Works Cited.

How to use · Practice path steps:
1 Name place and audience: One line each; keep them stable through the draft.
2 Write the how/why: Cut any version that takes a side.
3 Fill the plate: All seven labels before paragraph one of the essay.
4 Log 1 to 3 sources: Each source has a job that matches a subtopic.
5 Draft from the plate: Do not invent a new topic in paragraph two.
6 Revise: Cut side-taking, source dumping, and blank plate labels.

AI Level 2 note: AI may help list plate labels or give feedback after you have notes. The plate and the submitted sentences are yours.

### tools/index.html

Eyebrow/context: Case Study practice helpers

LEAD: Helpers for the Case Study essay. Open a tool to draft a practice thesis, draft topic sentences, build search keywords, practice citations, log sources, or rehearse peer feedback. Each page explains how to use that tool.

Tool card blurbs (light consistency):
- Thesis / claim generator: Fill packet fields to get 2 to 4 draft theses you can copy, edit, and carry into the outline.
- MLA citation generator: Pick source type (web, article, journal, book, film, packet) to practice Works Cited and in-text. Verify every entry.
- Outline / topic sentences: Carry your thesis to draft topic sentences for framing, stakeholders, evidence, assumptions, limits, plus outside-source slots.
- Source log: Track packet plus 3 to 4 outside sources: notes, how each helps the thesis, MLA draft slots. Copy a summary anytime.
- Peer-review checklist: Rehearsal for thesis, packet plus outside sources, MLA, organization, AI Level 2 honesty: checkboxes plus notes.
- Keyword generator: Topic, thesis, or case to keywords, synonyms, Boolean tips, Scholar and library query drafts. Local practice only.
- Case Study workspace: Full brainstorm, notes, outline, draft, checklist.

Tool page LEADs (already passed earlier; keep unless you need re-paste):
- thesis-helper: Fill in what you noticed in your Source Packet / Case Brief. This tool builds 2 to 4 draft thesis options from structured templates (AI Level 2; no chatbot). You own the thesis: edit it, verify it against the packet, then carry it into the outline helper or Case Study studio.
- outline-helper: Paste or auto-carry the thesis you chose. This helper drafts a 1 to 2 page outline skeleton with topic-sentence starters for Case Study lenses (problem or framing, stakeholders, evidence, assumptions, limits) plus slots for 3 to 4 outside sources. Structured templates only; edit everything in your voice.
- mla-citation: Pick a source type, fill the fields you have, and build a practice Works Cited entry (hanging-indent preview) plus a sample in-text citation. Templates only; no chatbot. You own accuracy: verify every entry against the source and Purdue OWL or your handbook.
- source-log: Track your Source Packet / Case Brief and the 3 to 4 outside sources your Case Study needs. Log what each source says, how it helps your thesis, and a rough MLA draft, then polish citations in the MLA tool.
- peer-review-checklist: Rehearse feedback on a Case Study draft (about 1,000 to 1,500 words MLA, packet plus 3 to 4 outside sources, AI Level 2). Check what is working, leave a short note, then copy or print a summary for your partner.
- keyword-generator: Turn your topic, thesis, or case into search keywords, synonyms, Boolean tip strings, and draft queries for Google Scholar and the SAC Library. Templates run on your device; you still evaluate every source (packet plus 3 to 4 outside).
