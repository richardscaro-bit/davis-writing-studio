# ENGL 2328 Sp26 — Introduction to Our Final Project
URL: https://alamo.instructure.com/courses/1719888/pages/introduction-to-our-final-project

Welcome to the first steps towards your final project in our exploration of American horror literature. This project allows you to immerse yourself deeply in the themes, styles, and narrative techniques we will discuss throughout the course, culminating in a comprehensive and creative exploration of the genre.

## Objective of the Final Project
Your final project will be an opportunity to synthesize and apply your understanding of American literature's critical themes, narrative techniques, and stylistic elements. The aim is to create a project reflecting your analytical skills and creative engagement with the course materials.

## Options for the Final Project
- **Analytical Essay:** In-depth essay on a topic related to American horror literature's themes, motifs, or stylistic elements; a particular author, specific work, or broader theme spanning multiple works.
- **Creative Writing Piece:** A short story, play, or series of poems that incorporates and reflects upon the elements of American horror literature discussed in class; clearly understands the genre's conventions and themes.
- **Multimedia Presentation:** Video, podcast, or digital presentation analyzing an essential aspect of American horror literature (author biographies, thematic explorations, adaptations into other media).
- **Comparative Study:** Comparative analysis of two or more works from the course, on similar themes or different narrative techniques.
- **Student's Choice:** Propose your own project aligned with the course focus (art project, series of blog posts, literary critique, etc.). Proposal should outline the project's nature and how it relates to course themes and objectives.

## Preparation Steps for Week 1
- Start by Exploring Ideas: reflect on this week's stories and discussions. What themes or elements in "An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge" and "The Tell-Tale Heart" intrigued you?
- Initial Research: begin researching potential topics; deeper reading into Bierce and Poe or related authors.
- Brainstorming: jot down ideas; consider how elements discussed in class could be woven into your chosen format.
- Proposal Outline: brief outline/proposal including chosen format, potential topics or themes, and approach.

## Guidelines for Success
- Stay Engaged: participate in discussions and activities.
- Consult Resources: course materials, additional readings, external resources.
- Seek Feedback: discuss ideas with peers.
- Be Creative and Analytical.

Your final project is not just a culmination of what you've learned; it's a personal exploration of the fascinating world of American horror literature. Begin thinking about it early.
