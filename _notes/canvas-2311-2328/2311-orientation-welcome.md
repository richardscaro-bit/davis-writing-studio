Course Welcome

Welcome to ENGL 2311!
Hello students,

Welcome to the fascinating world of Technical Writing! This is ENGL 2311, and I am delighted to guide you
through the intricate and vital aspects of technical communication. This course is not just about writing; it's
about understanding your audience, presenting complex information in digestible formats, and equipping
yourself with skills that will be invaluable in your professional journey.

Course Overview: Throughout this semester, we will dive deep into four major assignments:

    1. Audience Analysis: Understanding your readers is the bedrock of effective communication. In this
       assignment, you'll delve into the psyche of your intended audience, learning to tailor your message
       accordingly.

    2. The Proposal: A well-crafted proposal can make or break a project. Here, you'll learn the art of
       persuasive and concise writing, presenting solutions in a structured and compelling manner.

    3. Formal Report Writing: A staple in the technical world, mastering the formal report equips you with the
       ability to convey comprehensive information in a coherent and professional format.

    4. The Job Application: Whether you're aiming for your dream job or an exciting internship, this
       assignment will provide insights into crafting the perfect resume, cover letter, and other essential
       application materials.

Expectations:

       Engagement: Active participation is crucial. Join discussions, ask questions, and engage with your
       peers.

       Quality: Put in the time and effort to produce well-researched and carefully crafted assignments.

       Timeliness: Deadlines are non-negotiable. Punctuality in submissions reflects professionalism.

       Integrity: Plagiarism will not be tolerated. Ensure all work is original and properly cited.

Success Strategies:

    1. Start Early: Give yourself ample time for research, drafting, revising, and proofreading.

    2. Seek Feedback: Whether it's from peers, the writing center, or me, constructive criticism is a treasure.
    3. Stay Organized: Regularly check Canvas for updates, keep track of deadlines, and maintain orderly
       notes.

    4. Practice Makes Perfect: The more you write, the better you become. Don't be afraid to rewrite and
       refine.

    5. Stay Curious: Explore additional resources, read widely, and immerse yourself in the world of technical
       writing.

I am excited to embark on this journey with each of you. Together, we'll discover the various aspects of effective
technical communication, fostering skills that will serve you for years.

Best wishes for a productive and enlightening semester!

Caroline Davis



Course Description
Intensive study of and practice in the strategies and techniques for developing research-based expository and
persuasive texts. Emphasis on effective and ethical rhetorical inquiry, including primary and secondary research
methods; critical reading of verbal, visual, and multimedia texts; systematic evaluation, synthesis, and
documentation of information sources; and critical thinking about evidence and conclusions. This course fulfills
the Communication foundational component area of the core and addresses the following required objectives:
Critical Thinking, Communication, Teamwork, and Personal Responsibility



Prerequisite(s)
Students must earn a "C" or better in ENGL 1301 or its equivalent.
