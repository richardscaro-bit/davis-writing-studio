For your first major essay, delve into the intricate themes of reality and illusion as presented in American horror literature. Drawing upon our readings so far - including works by Ambrose Bierce, Edgar Allan Poe, Henry James, Edith Wharton, Charles W. Chesnutt, Mary E. Wilkins Freeman, H.P. Lovecraft, F. Scott Fitzgerald, William Faulkner, Zora Neale Hurston, and Jean Toomer - select one or two stories that particularly resonate with you. Your essay should explore how these authors use narrative techniques to blur the lines between reality and illusion and the impact this has on the reader's experience.

## Objectives
- To analyze how selected works utilize the theme of reality versus illusion.
- To examine the narrative techniques contributing to this theme, such as unreliable narrators, the manipulation of time, and the use of supernatural or surreal elements.
- To explore the broader implications of this theme in the context of American horror literature.

## Essay Requirements
- **Length: between 1,500 and 2,000 words.**
- Structure: Include an introduction with a thesis statement, body paragraphs with supporting arguments and evidence from the text(s), and a conclusion that ties your analysis together.
- Analysis: Provide a detailed exploration of how the theme of reality versus illusion is presented in your chosen story or stories. Discuss the techniques used by the author(s) to create this theme and how it affects the reader's narrative perception.
- Citations: Use proper citations for direct quotes or paraphrased ideas, adhering to MLA or APA format.

## Suggestions for Writing Your Essay
- Introduction: Begin with a brief overview of the theme of reality versus illusion in American horror literature. Introduce your chosen work(s) and your thesis statement.
- Narrative Techniques: Analyze specific aspects of your chosen story or stories that blur the lines between reality and illusion (unreliable narrators, the supernatural, manipulation of time and perception).
- Impact on Reader: Discuss how these techniques affect the reader's experience. Do they create suspense, ambiguity, confusion, or a sense of dread?
- Broader Context: Consider how the theme fits within the broader context of American horror literature. What does it reveal about the human experience, societal fears, or the nature of horror storytelling?
- Conclusion: Summarize your main points and reiterate how your analysis supports your thesis.
- References: List all the sources you have used in your research.
