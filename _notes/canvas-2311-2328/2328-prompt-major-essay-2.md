Your second major essay invites you to delve into the thematic richness of 20th-century American literature by examining the works of Shirley Jackson, Richard Matheson, Ray Bradbury, Sylvia Plath, or Flannery O'Connor. Through a focused analysis of one chosen theme—such as identity, alienation, the American Dream, or the confrontation with societal norms—you will explore how these author(s) articulate and examine human experiences within their unique cultural and historical contexts.

## Objectives
- Identify key ideas, representative authors and works, significant historical or cultural events, and characteristic perspectives or attitudes expressed in the literature of different periods or regions.
- Analyze literary works as expressions of individual or communal values within the social, political, cultural, or religious contexts of different literary periods.
- Demonstrate knowledge of the development of characteristic forms or styles of expression during different historical periods or in different regions.
- Articulate the aesthetic principles that guide the scope and variety of works in the arts and humanities.
- Write research-based critical papers about the assigned readings in clear and grammatically correct prose, using various critical approaches to literature.

## Essay Requirements
- **Length: 1500-2000 words.**
- Format: MLA guidelines for formatting, citations, references.
- Sources: at least three scholarly secondary sources offering critical perspectives on the chosen works or theme.
- Submission: upload PDF or Word to the submission portal.
- Deadline (as printed): April 7, 2024 (stale year; Canvas due Apr 19)

## Instructions
1. Select Two Authors: from Shirley Jackson, Richard Matheson, Ray Bradbury, Sylvia Plath, Flannery O'Connor, and Truman Capote. One significant work per author that best explores a central theme of your choosing.
2. Thematic Analysis: identify and articulate the theme connecting your works; concise summary of each work showing how the theme develops; analyze narrative techniques (character development, setting, plot); examine historical and cultural backdrop.
3. Comparative Synthesis: compare and contrast the authors' approaches; synthesize to comment on the theme's broader significance in American literature and culture and what the works reveal about societal attitudes of their periods.
4. Contemporary Relevance and Personal Reflection: how the theme resonates with or differs from today's issues; personal reflection on the works' impact on your understanding of the theme and current discussions on society and identity.

Note: Assignment shell objectives mirror these; Final Project Due assignment reuses the same four objectives (Thematic Analysis, Contextual Understanding, Comparative Insight, Reflective Engagement).
