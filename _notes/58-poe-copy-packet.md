### weeks.html
LEAD: A short map of what each Case Study week is for and where to practice. Path: in-class demo → brainstorm → lock a working thesis in Week 13 → draft → one peer review → polished essay. Open a week card for studio and Tools links.
SOFT PURPOSE: One clear claim + packet evidence (Source Packet / Case Brief) + 3–4 outside sources beyond the packet. Aim for a 1,000–1,500 word MLA critical analysis (AI Level 2: support, not composition).
WEEK: What this week is for: see one case demo in class (BarkBox / Crumbl models), pick a topic, and brainstorm early ideas. Your case Source Packet and Case Brief are the required unit readings. Do not lock a thesis yet — idea generation first. Practice in the Case Study studio topic picker and brainstorm workspace.
WEEK: What this week is for: active reading of your Source Packet / Case Brief — problem, stakeholders, evidence, assumptions/biases, limits. Keep notes you can reuse in the outline and draft. Practice reading notes in the studio; log sources in the Source log tool.
WEEK: What this week is for: shape a 1–2 page outline and research plan with a working thesis/claim, and identify 3–4 outside sources beyond the packet. This is when the claim locks after demo + brainstorm. Practice with the thesis, outline, and source-log tools, or draft the outline in the studio.
WEEK: What this week is for: grow a working draft toward full essay length, integrate or placeholder the 3–4 outside sources, and rehearse one peer review. Practice drafting in the studio; use the peer-review checklist and MLA practice tools to prepare feedback and citations.
WEEK: What this week is for: finish a 1,000–1,500 word MLA critical analysis (Works Cited not in word count) with packet evidence primary and 3–4 outside sources beyond the packet. Use the final checklist in the Case Study studio, the MLA generator, and the AI Level 2 guide (support, not composition).

### case-study-studio.html
LEAD: A practice room for the Case Study critical analysis — pick a topic, open packet readings, then work brainstorm → notes → outline → draft. Rehearse one peer review and run the final checklist when you are ready. Jump links below open each studio block; Tools and Resources stay one click away.
SOFT PURPOSE: One clear claim + packet evidence (Source Packet / Case Brief) + 3–4 outside sources beyond the packet. Aim for a 1,000–1,500 word MLA critical analysis (AI Level 2: support, not composition).

### tools/index.html
LEAD: Helpers for the Case Study essay. Open a tool to generate a practice claim, draft topic sentences, build search keywords, practice citations, log sources, or rehearse peer feedback. Each page explains how to use that tool.

### tools/thesis-helper.html
LEAD: Fill in what you noticed in your Source Packet / Case Brief. This tool builds 2–4 draft claim options from structured templates (AI Level 2 — no chatbot). You own the claim: edit it, verify it against the packet, then carry it into the outline helper or Case Study studio.

### tools/outline-helper.html
LEAD: Paste or auto-carry the claim you chose. This helper drafts a 1–2 page outline skeleton with topic-sentence starters for Case Study lenses (problem/framing, stakeholders, evidence, assumptions, limits) plus slots for 3–4 outside sources. Structured templates only — edit everything in your voice.

### tools/mla-citation.html
LEAD: Pick a source type, fill the fields you have, and build a practice Works Cited entry (hanging-indent preview) plus a sample in-text citation. Templates only — no chatbot. You own accuracy: verify every entry against the source and Purdue OWL / your handbook.

### tools/source-log.html
LEAD: Track your Source Packet / Case Brief and the 3–4 outside sources your Case Study needs. Log what each source says, how it helps your claim, and a rough MLA draft — then polish citations in the MLA tool.

### tools/peer-review-checklist.html
LEAD: Rehearse feedback on a Case Study draft (about 1k–1.5k MLA · packet + 3–4 outside sources · AI Level 2). Check what is working, leave a short note, then copy or print a summary for your partner.

### tools/keyword-generator.html
LEAD: Turn your topic, claim, or case into search keywords, synonyms, Boolean-ish tip strings, and draft queries for Google Scholar and the SAC Library. Templates run on your device — you still evaluate every source (packet + 3–4 outside).
