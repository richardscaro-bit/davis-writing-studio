Monster Cards Comparison Studio

A practice room for the Monster Cards comparison. Pick two monsters that share meaningful ground, build one character card for each, then draft a reflection essay that uses the cards as primary evidence. Jump links below open Tools and handouts; keep AI at Level 2 (support, not composition).

Purpose

Compare two monsters through shared criteria and one insight beyond a list. Build two character cards, then a 750 to 1,000 word MLA reflection that uses the cards as primary evidence (AI Level 2: support, not composition).

What each character card needs

Make one card per monster (digital or by hand). Keep language tight. Match categories across both cards so your comparison stays fair.

  • Name / aliases
  • Origin / source (text, film, folklore, or cultural context)
  • Appearance
  • Abilities / powers
  • Weaknesses / vulnerabilities
  • Motivations / drives
  • Symbolic or thematic resonance
  • Key detail (short quotation or defining detail)

Optional reverse side: a short note on why the figure matters. Clarity and visual hierarchy matter more than decoration.

How to use this studio

Practice path

1Pick a pairTwo monsters from literature, film, folklore, myth, or media that share meaningful comparative ground. 2Build both cardsFill the same categories on each card so criteria stay consistent. 3Lock a working thesisName the insight the comparison reveals — not only a list of similarities and differences. 4Outline the reflectionPlan 750 to 1,000 words MLA with the cards as primary evidence. 5Rehearse feedbackRun one peer pass on insight, criteria, and card-to-essay coherence. 6Polish MLAPractice citations and Works Cited; verify every entry against your sources.
AI Level 2

AI may help brainstorm card layouts or give reader-style feedback after you have your own notes or draft. AI may not write or rewrite submitted cards, analysis, thesis, or the finished essay. Keep a short disclosure if you use AI in a permitted way.
