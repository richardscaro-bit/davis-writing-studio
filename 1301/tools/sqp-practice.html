Citing this source in MLA 9

Every summary, paraphrase, and quote needs an in-text citation. MLA 9 gives you two forms. The source has three authors, so the short name is Siddik et al.

Narrative citation

You name the authors in your own sentence. The parentheses hold only the page number.

Pattern: name the authors, give the idea or quote, then (2).

Parenthetical citation

You do not name the authors in your sentence. The parentheses hold the author and the page together, with no comma.

Pattern: give the idea or quote, then (Siddik et al. 2).

Four rules for every citation

  1. Use the page number from the passage. This passage is on page 2.
  2. Narrative form is . Parenthetical form is .
  3. Do not put a comma between the author and the page. Do not add p., page, or the year.
  4. The period comes after the closing parenthesis, not before it.

Do not use both forms at once. If you write "Siddik et al. state that" in your sentence, the citation is just (2). A paraphrase or a summary needs a citation too. Put it at the end of the idea, as the handout examples do.

See the citation mistakes this page flags
  • No citation, or no page number.
  • A comma, as in (Siddik et al., 2), or et al without its period.
  • The author named in the sentence and again in the parentheses.
  • Two citations in a row, or a citation with the wrong page.
  • A period before the citation instead of after it.

How the labels are decided

Strong means every check passed. Medium means the must-haves are there and something needs polish. Weak means a must-have is missing or wrong. The checker only looks at the words you typed. It cannot judge meaning the way a teacher can, so use it as a guide.

See every check

All four parts

  • Must-have: an in-text citation with page 2, in a form MLA 9 accepts, and the authors named in your sentence or in the parentheses.
  • Polish: narrative form (2) when you name the authors, parenthetical form (Siddik et al. 2) when you do not, no comma, no doubled citation, and the period after the closing parenthesis.

Summary

  • Must-have: shorter than the passage, mentions at least two main ideas, not copied word for word.
  • Polish: about 90 percent of the passage length or less, names water, carbon, and data centers, no quotation marks, no opinion.

Paraphrase

  • Must-have: at least 25 words, at least two of the three ideas, and not patchwriting. Patchwriting means 30 percent or more of your four word groups match the passage, or a copied run of seven words. Technical terms such as water footprint, blue water, and global warming potential are not counted.
  • Polish: all three ideas, less than 15 percent match, no quotation marks.

Quote

  • Must-have: quotation marks in pairs, and the quoted words found in the passage.
  • Polish: capitalization and punctuation exactly as in the passage, a signal phrase before the quote, a quote of 35 words or fewer, and the period outside the quotation marks.

Quote sandwich

  • Introduce: names the authors, sets up the idea, and keeps the quote and citation for the next part.
  • Quote: the same checks as the Quote step. The authors can be named in the Introduce part.
  • Explain: comes after the quote, uses your own words, says what the quote shows, and connects it to a point. It is not a repeat of the quote.
  • The whole paragraph gets the lowest label of its three parts.
