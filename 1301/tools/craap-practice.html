This is practice only

This page is for practice today. It is not graded, and it is not required for your Case Study essay. Nothing you write here has to be turned in.

Use it to look closely at your case study packet, which is your primary source. The CRAAP Test is a short list of questions that can help you judge how much you can trust a source. Work through the five letters below. For each one, look back at your packet and write a short answer in your own words.

Practice only

Type your answers in the boxes. They are saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing has to be turned in.

Currency: The timeliness of the information

  1. When was your case study packet published, posted, or last updated?
  2. Does your analysis need recent information, or can older packet details still work well?
  3. If the packet includes dates, news, or data, do those still feel timely for the issue you are analyzing?

Write your answer. Note the date you found, and say whether the packet still feels timely enough for your essay.

Relevance: The importance of the information for your needs

  1. Which parts of the packet relate most clearly to the question or claim you want to make?
  2. Who seems to be the intended audience of the packet?
  3. Would you be comfortable citing this packet as your primary source in the essay?

Write your answer. Name the packet parts you will use, and say why they fit your essay.

Authority: The source of the information

  1. Who created, published, or sponsored the materials in your packet?
  2. What credentials or affiliations can you find for those authors or organizations?
  3. Based on that, how qualified do they seem to speak on this topic?

Write your answer. Name the author or organization, and say why you do or do not trust their authority.

Accuracy: The reliability, truthfulness, and correctness of the content

  1. Where does the information in the packet come from, and is it supported by evidence?
  2. Can you verify any packet details in another source or from what you already know?
  3. Does the language or tone seem careful and fair, or does it lean on emotion?

Write your answer. Point to one detail you can check, and say how accurate the packet seems.

Purpose: The reason the information exists

  1. What seems to be the purpose of the packet: to inform, teach, sell, entertain, or persuade?
  2. Do the authors or sponsors make that purpose clear?
  3. Does the point of view seem fairly objective, or do you notice a bias you should name in your essay?

Write your answer. State the purpose you see, and note any bias that might shape the packet.

Wrap-up

What did this check tell you about how much you can trust your source?

In two or three sentences, say what you can rely on in the packet, and what you should treat with care.
